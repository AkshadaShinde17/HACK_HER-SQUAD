🪴 Soil review

Record observations and lab results. A photograph alone cannot measure nutrients.

Take a close-up in natural daylight. Avoid filters and include a clean patch of soil.

How to use this result

Soil type and moisture are field observations. A soil lab report is needed to assess nutrient status and salinity.

If pH is below 5.5, ask a soil laboratory or agronomist whether acidity affects your crop. If above 7.5, confirm alkalinity and crop-specific management before adding amendments. These are screening flags, not a fertiliser prescription.

OBSERVE EARLY

🛡️ Crop health & symptom checker

Select your crop and symptoms to see possible causes and safe next steps. You can also upload a photo for a basic visible-colour scan; it is not a trained disease detector.

Use a sharp close-up of the affected leaf and, if possible, one whole-plant photo. Keep the leaf in focus.

Before treating a crop

  1. Check both affected and healthy plants.
  2. Note which leaves or plant parts are affected.
  3. Check root-zone moisture and recent weather.
  4. Photograph the pattern and note whether it is spreading.
  5. Verify the cause with a KVK or agriculture officer before applying chemicals.
The disease name is a symptom-based shortlist, not laboratory confirmation. Do not spray based on a name alone; verify the cause with your local KVK or agriculture officer.

Quick checklist before applying

  • Confirm your state, district and current season.
  • Read eligibility and exclusion criteria on the official website.
  • Check the latest deadline and notified crops/areas where relevant.
  • Never pay an unofficial agent or share OTPs/passwords with anyone.
  • If unsure, contact your Gram Panchayat, agriculture office, KVK or bank.
